01

The determinism boundary

One part of the entire system is not deterministic. Everything else is arithmetic over stored facts. chess.js decides legality. Stockfish decides truth: how much better a position is, whether a forced mate exists, and the best line. Maia decides what a human of a given rating would actually play. The annotator decides what mattered: verdict tiers, turning points, conversion failures, game phase. Claude only puts it into words.

From the spec: code computes every chess fact, award and schedule; the model only phrases them.

Four texts reach me, and where each appears does not say who wrote it. The hint ladder under the board and the debrief after the game are written by code, from templates filled with stored facts. The coach writes two things: its chat replies and the one-line note under the hint ladder. Those are the only sentences a model composes.

SO

A false line in a debrief is my own template contradicting my own data. server/annotator/ and src/review/ make zero model calls, so nothing there can invent a fact; a code search confirms it. Where the coach does write, its words are checked before I see them, and which checks run depends on where: §06 shows each check against board chat, general strategy chat and the coach's note under the hint ladder.

02

What crosses the boundary

A typed fact list, not a game history. It is handed the answers. Two assembly paths, and both deliberately skip the live evaluator so the coach can never contend with the judge's Stockfish queue.

Position

  • currentFen the live board
  • toMove whose turn it is
  • occupancy every piece, every square
  • legalSans every legal move
  • contested attackers and defenders

Move

  • gameSans every move played
  • allowedSans what may be named
  • side whose move it was, written on the row when the move is made, never worked out from its place in the list

Engine

  • perPlyAnalysis eval, best, line, phase
  • hintFindings what the hint search already found and verified for this position
  • read the eval, as words
  • gap played versus best, in words
  • contestedAfterBest which squares are attacked and defended once the best move is played. Added after game 198, when the coach described attacks on the board before the move it was explaining.
  • candidateLine a move I name in chat but have not played gets its own checked engine line, instead of a guess

UI state -- what I am looking at

  • pendingMove the piece I picked up
  • hintFocus the open hint, verbatim
  • turningPointFocus the card I clicked
  • highlightedPlies moves I flagged
  • recentHints the deep hints I was shown this game, oldest first, at most eight, so the coach no longer guesses which ones I saw

Turning points

  • turningPoints ply, move, label
  • focusPosition the board as it was then

History

  • status live or finished
  • outcome result, winner, how
  • standingNotes cross-game memory

The direction matters. Facts go in, phrases come back. The model never writes a fact back into the store.

Two consequences. Raw evaluations never reach the model as numbers; they are bucketed into words first, because a coach that says "plus 144" to a beginner is useless. And the pending move is distrusted: the client claims it, the server replays it on a probe, and drops it if it is illegal.

04

Two surfaces, one brain

The worst bug here was plumbing, not the model. Two surfaces search the same position. The judge runs a 350ms evaluation on every move I consider, and that is what holds the sub-two-second verdict. The hint runs a 1500ms multi-line search plus a 500ms verification pass, escalating to 3000ms. Rare, and I have to ask for it.

From the spec: a chat answer is grounded in engine facts, and thumbs-down captures why it missed.

TimeGame 167, move 19, 2026-08-02 -- every row from the database
11:18The hint engine found a forced mate, verified it, and showed it to me on screen.
11:21I asked the coach about it. It timed out and served a canned template.
11:22I asked again. It replied: "no mate line for this position in the facts i've got."

"The hint said that there's a forced mate that's available if I move my knight to G5. When I tried to ask the coach about it, the coach couldn't figure it out and gave a timed-out answer. Now, even after feeding it to the coach and telling it to check the chess facts from stockfish, the coach is claiming that the chess brain has not found a forced mate, but I know that it has."

my playtest note, 2026-08-02

The cause? The hint computed the mate, used the score to pick which piece to point at, then threw it away at the return. The mate distance was never carried. The coach's facts had ten categories and zero mentions of mate. The mate checker's set of true mate distances was empty, so nothing even blocked a mate claim. The model correctly refused to invent one.

SO

The coach and the hint did not share a brain. Two searches, two answers, one of them thrown away. The fix publishes the hint's verified findings to a keyed shelf, folded into the coach's facts only on an exact position match. What I actually asked for: "pass it to the coach as its own shortlist, so it doesn't need to go through every move on the chess facts."

Two later changes closed what that left open. When I ask the coach about a position the hint has not searched yet, the coach now runs the hint's own verified search first and keeps the result for that position, so the chat and the hint never answer from two different searches. And when I name a move in chat that I have not picked up on the board, the coach works out that move's line the same verified way instead of guessing at it.

05

Routing: this board, or chess in general

Before any model call, code decides: this position, or chess in general? A board question gets everything. A general question gets a fact list of four items and no position at all.

From the spec: when I do not understand why a move was wrong, I ask in plain English and get an answer at my level.

It began as a thumbs-down: "will only answer about moves i already did, not general chess questions for strategy next game." The refusal was coming from a line in the prompt, not from any check. So the decision moved into code, before the call. Deterministic, and that was my explicit choice, because model self-routing was the failure I had already hit.

Three regex tiers, zero model calls. If I am pointing at something on screen, it is a board question, unconditionally. Otherwise a general-phrase test. Otherwise an abstract-theory test that fires only when there is no board signal at all. Ambiguity resolves to board.

The first version had it backwards. It routed to board only when it detected a board signal. 38 of 65 real board questions carry no detectable signal. "is this ok". "should i take it". A bare "yes". All of them fell through to the general route, which has no position on it. Exactly the trust failure the round existed to fix. So board became the unconditional default.

SHIP GATE

A read-only tool replayed the old and the new routing over 102 of my real messages from the database, before merge. Zero questions flipped from board to general.

06

What the model is not allowed to say

Nine checks, and which ones run depends on where the coach writes: board chat, general strategy chat, or the coach's note under the hint ladder. The chart below lists each one. Each check has a plain name, the name it carries in the code, and the one rule it enforces.

From the spec's ship gates: zero invented moves; a failed fact-check pauses the coach, never the game.

Which checks run where

One check is new since this page was first written, and two were widened. Since September 21 a capture or attack the coach describes must be possible on the board. The check on where pieces stand now reads every board the coach was shown, up to four moves down the line it is explaining, instead of the current board alone. Since September 22 the voice check also catches a reply that corrects itself mid-sentence or leaks an internal label from its fact list. A separate rule bans "quiet" for a move, because it reads as a language-model tic (the fourth technical decision).

The coach's note under the hint ladder runs three checks: the moves it names, the squares it names, and which pieces are protected. The square list is stricter than anything chat runs: every square it names must come from the facts it was handed (the threat and the recommended move). So a note cannot place a piece on a square it was not told about, even without the placement check. What it does not yet check is in the chart's last caption line.

A failed check triggers one corrective retry. A second failure falls back to a canned reply from the persona file. Never a sentence that failed its own check, and never a blocked game.

ECONOMICS

The prose checks are cheap: 0.013 ms and 0.070 ms when I timed them in July. A retry is a second full model call, roughly 20 seconds more. Across the 290 model-written replies stored between July 18 and September 22, 9.66% needed one. So adding a check is free, and cutting the retry rate is the only lever worth pulling. That is why the correction for mallow's move quality fixes the wording in place at no cost instead of triggering a retry. The placement fix in the next section mattered for the same reason: in game 198, 14 of the check's 16 rejections threw out true answers.

07

Proving a fix on past games

I do not wait for new games to find out whether a fix worked. A fix runs against every position already stored, before and after the change, and then I break it on purpose to prove the harness can tell the difference.

From the spec: a red flag is something to fix, not a feature to cut, and diagnosis starts from my thumbs-down notes and the traces.

In game 198 the check on where pieces stand rejected 16 coach answers. 14 were true statements about the position after the move being explained; the check had compared them to the board before it. The easy fix was to delete the check. I kept it and taught it which boards the coach was talking about, and it still catches the 2 answers that were wrong.

A check only ever seen passing proves nothing. So I broke the fix on purpose: pulling the move lines back out brought the after-move rejections straight back, and restoring them cleared them again. Four such breaks ran, each built to move one named cell of the results and nothing else.

Equal mates now count as the best move

The same method proved a second fix. In game 198 my move mated as fast as the engine's stored best move, and the review still marked it as a miss, because it compared the two by name rather than by how fast they mate. The fix treats an equally fast mate as the best move everywhere the review judges a move.

The judge's own nudge about a faster mate got the matching rule. It fires only when the mate I held before my move was 8 moves away or fewer, and it counts both mates from the same point. Before that, 17 of the 60 stored faster-mate nudges had printed the same number twice ("mate in N was there, now it's mate in N" with the same N); 8 of them from one position.

WHAT IT CANNOT CATCH YET

The placement check now accepts a claim that is true on any board the coach was shown, up to four moves down a line. A claim about the current board that only becomes true a few moves later would pass, and the "false statements caught" figures above are measured against that rule. Nothing yet measures how often the coach writes one in play.

08

How we set thinking and effort

Two independent controls, not one parameter. thinking: {type:"disabled"} turns adaptive thinking off. A separate top-level effort: "low" caps it while leaving it on. Three arms, 119 fixed questions, three repetitions each, 357 samples per arm.

Latency by arm

Fallback by difficulty

So why low? Difficulty, not the average. On the hardest bucket it beats both. It beats disabled in every bucket at a speed cost inside the noise, and beats default on tactical questions while running 2.3x faster on a fifth of the tokens. A blinded human read of 26 questions, columns randomized and the key held separately, gave the bounded arms 18 of 25 non-tie picks.

NOTE

The plan going in said apply this to live chat only. The branch review called the missing gate a bug. The eval overturned both, and it shipped everywhere including postgame review, where low scored 21% fallback against default's 29%.

09

Finding that root cause took four passes

Twelve days, four investigations. The first three each found a real cause, shipped a real fix, watched the median improve, and filed the rest under model variance.

PassDiagnosisRight?
Jul 21Process startup, not thinking timeReal, and the fix was real
Jul 27Budgets are too tightA mitigation, not a diagnosis
Aug 1Stale served code, starved retry cap, prompt sizeTwo real. The third was a confound
Aug 2Unbounded adaptive thinking, 1,500-4,500 invisible tokensNamed, reproduced, toggled

Anatomy of one call at the low end of that range, 20.3 seconds: 1.3s to the first message, then thinking from 1.7s to 17.7s spending 1,430 tokens, then 2.4 seconds of actual writing. The slow ones ran past 50.

It hid because thinking streams with empty text by default. No log, no trace, no stream ever showed a thinking token. The two tells were arithmetic and both sat in every call since July 22: time-to-first-token of 1.3-3.7 seconds against 45-second waits, and billed output tokens far exceeding the visible answer.

LESSON

A fix that measurably helps is the most dangerous stopping point. It validates the pass without validating the diagnosis.

10

Two engines, because truth and human-likeness are different jobs

Stockfish evaluates. Maia opposes, playing like a human of a given rating. A game instead of a wall.

From the spec: opponents that play human-like at every level up to Maia's 1900 cap.

Two details cost real debugging. Maia runs at Temperature = 1.0. At the engine default of 0 it always plays its single most likely move, which makes it play stronger than its rating band. Real 1100-rated players sample the mistakes too. And we send ucinewgame before every query, because the sampling randomness is not reseeded per move. A warmed instance once returned the same move 40 times out of 40.

Search budgets split on purpose. The judge gets 350ms, run twice, which is what holds the sub-two-second verdict. A hint gets 1500ms plus a 500ms verification pass, because hints served from the judge's shallow eval failed playtest.

11

The interface that made the coach swappable

CoachBackend is an interface with four members, one optional. It was designed before there was a performance problem to solve.

From the spec: the game never depends on a subscription to run; a local model sits behind the same interface.

Then telemetry showed 13 of 14 live nudges missing their own 15-second budget. That budget had to cover process startup, and startup ate most of every call. The fix was a second implementation of the same interface. Nothing downstream changed. Narration went 9s to 3.6s, chat 9-13s to 7s.

Every growth since has been an additive optional parameter, so the other three backends needed zero edits each time.

Verdicts outnumber moves: every move I considered and retracted was judged and recorded too. Of the chat messages, 201 are mine and 176 are the coach's. There is no migration folder and no version number: the code declares every column each table should have, diffs that against the live database at startup, and adds what is missing.

Whose move it was is recorded, not computed. The party that made a move (me or mallow) is written onto its row the moment the move is made, read straight off the chess engine's own move object; nothing downstream ever derives whose move it was from a ply number.
