CHART 3

Do retries actually rescue answers?

You said "I've never seen the regen work." When an answer runs long, the system retries once. Here is what those retries produced — at the old 45-second limit, and at the new 3-minute budget.
TAKEAWAY

Retrying was never broken — the 45-second limit starved it. Give a retry room to finish and, at normal thinking, every stalled answer was rescued (10 of 10) — up from 9 of 21 at the old limit. The regen you never saw work now works.

SURPRISE

At a 3-minute budget even normal thinking had zero give-ups. Raising the budget alone erased the timeout failures — so the thinking limit is a speed lever, not a rescue lever. (The two bounded arms show 4 give-ups each, but those are the fact-check failures of Chart 5, not timeouts.)

Source: the three overnight 3-minute runs (65 questions each) vs the 45-second baseline. Retries counted as regens that fired; "rescued" = ended as a real answer, "starved" = ended as an apology.

CHART 5

What the fix costs

Capping the thinking makes answers faster — but does a faster answer become a wrong answer? Both speed and fact-check failures are now measured.
TAKEAWAY

The speed win is about 3× at the median — and it tames the 64-second tail to 15 — but it has a real cost. Bounding the thinking made roughly 1 in 16 first drafts (6.2%) fail the coach's fact-checks and turn into apologies, versus none at normal thinking. That argues for shipping the verified-facts work before or alongside the thinking limit, so the faster answers don't trade speed for being wrong.

STILL SEALED

The subjective, blinded quality read — is a limited-thinking answer actually as helpful to learn from? — is built but its answer key is unopened. Until it is read, "cap the thinking" is a strong speed lead, not a shipping decision.

Source: the three overnight 3-minute runs, 65 questions each. Speed = wall time to the answer; fact-check failures = first drafts rejected by the coach's own validators. Subjective grade: blinded worksheet built, key sealed.
