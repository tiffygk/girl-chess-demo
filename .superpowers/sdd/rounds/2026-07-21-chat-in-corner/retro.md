# Retrospective — chat-in-corner round (2026-07-21)

Scope: coach chat moved from a center modal into an inline panel in the coach's region, with provenance anchors on "ask about this." Three waves (thread-model logic, component-library build, port), one Opus review, one Fable visual gate. Merged to main as `b15bb14`, 589 tests green. This retro also covers two incidents that happened in the controller's window and are not visible in the ledger: a dev-stack kill during the visual gate, and eleven rendered iterations to land cookie's icon.

## What went well

- **The plan/execute split held.** Fable planned in its own window (writing the briefs into the ledger), a separate Opus window ran phases 2-4 with no manual model switching, and the ledger alone carried enough state for the Opus window to pick up cold. This is the first full round run under the rewritten `build-round` skill and the seam did not leak.
- **Parallel waves on disjoint files worked.** Wave 1 (logic, repo) and wave 2 (visual, vault-only component library) ran in parallel with zero file collision, because the plan explicitly scoped wave 2 to touch nothing in the repo.
- **The owner-approval pause did its job.** Nothing reached `src/` before the owner had seen and ruled on the component-library build (D1/D3/D7/opener copy). Wave 3 was gated on that approval in the brief, not just in intent.
- **Library-then-port pattern held again.** Same discipline the board itself was built under: prototype in the vault, get sign-off, port faithfully. Wave 3's report is explicit about porting the *approved* variant rather than re-deciding anything.
- **The round rebased cleanly onto a moving main.** Main advanced 5 commits (the persona/voice work, `49e2cc7`) while the round was in flight. The controller checked file overlap before assuming this was safe, found none, and it merged clean.
- **The controller verified independently instead of trusting reports.** Every wave's return was checked with `git log`, `tsc -b`, and `vitest run` in the worktree rather than taking the subagent's self-reported counts at face value. This is what caught the two icon renders the subagent had itself called done (see below) and confirmed report-3's claims before commit.
- **Decisions were recorded as they were made, with reasoning.** `decisions.md` is not just a verdict log — D1's revision states *why* the original "sidebar always" ruling was wrong (the controller's own earlier warning about 1512px being cramped turned out to be false once someone actually measured the board's CSS), and that reasoning is auditable by a future session without having to reconstruct it.
- **The review earned its keep.** One HIGH correctness bug (F1) and one real UX defect (F2) were caught pre-merge, both proven with a failing test first, per TDD discipline. The fix agent then caught itself reintroducing F2's exact cascade bug while fixing it, by reading the cascade instead of assuming the fix was correct. That second catch is the process working at both layers — reviewer and builder both checking their own claims against evidence rather than intuition.

## What went wrong

### 1. the stack kill (process failure, hardest rule broken)

The Phase 4 visual-gate subagent, cleaning up the alt-port servers it had started for the gate, ran a broad `pkill` and took down the owner's canonical dev stack on 5173/3001 — the single hardest rule in this codebase, because that stack may be live to a third party. The controller could not restart it (sandbox blocked the command); the owner had to restart it by hand.

This is a **process failure, not a judgment failure**. The brief said "never touch 5173/3001" and "kill what you start," but never said *how* to kill what you start. `pkill node` or `pkill vite` cannot distinguish the alt-port worktree stack from the canonical one — they are the same binaries. The instruction was correct in intent and incomplete in mechanism. Every brief written after this incident in the round carried an explicit "kill by PID only, NEVER pkill node or vite," which is the actual fix, not a restatement of the rule that already existed.

### 2. the icon (judgment failure, worst value-per-time in the round)

Cookie's glyph took eleven rendered iterations across three separate agent dispatches before it read as a fortune cookie: v1 mushroom, v2 tulip, v3 bowl-with-a-card, v4-v8 self-rejected (chopped mountain, coin, bean, mushroom, comet), v9-v11 finally landing a two-lobe anatomy. This was **not** a process failure — nothing was blocked, no infra broke — it was a judgment failure in how the controller directed the work for the first eight rounds.

What changed between failure and success: the controller stopped giving adjectives ("make it look like a fortune cookie") and started giving geometry — fat belly at the bottom, two unequal lobes split by a narrow crack, crease as an arc not a stem, slip angled out of the crack with its tip tucked. Paired with a hard self-test ("if bowl, taco, mushroom, tulip, shell, or croissant is a plausible read, it failed, iterate again") and an instruction to study real reference (OpenMoji/Twemoji/Noto 1F960) before drawing. All three of those were available on iteration one and were only reached for on iteration nine.

Also worth being honest about: the controller was, for most of those eleven rounds, forwarding the subagent's own self-assessment ("done") instead of rendering and judging the result itself. It caught two of the subagent's declared-done failures only once it started doing that judgment in its own context. The value delivered at the end does not really justify the cost: the owner accepted the final icon as texture at 26px plate size, on the explicit record that it does not read as a legible icon at that size, and the honest read in `decisions.md` is that the plate's own fortune slip was already doing that legibility job. Eleven iterations bought decoration, not comprehension.

### 3. what the review caught (the suite was green and still wrong)

Wave 1 shipped with 9 new tests and a fully green suite, and still shipped a HIGH-severity bug: `focusKey` was `hint:${level}:${text}` with no position component, and level-1 hint copy (`hold on. look at your knight.`) is a fixed template. Two different moments in the same game with the same hint level therefore collide on the same `focusKey`, so the second "ask about this" injects no anchor and her question lands under a stale one — the exact failure acceptance item 1 exists to prevent.

It survived wave 1 because every fixture in `chatThread.test.ts` used distinct text for every focus, so the collision case literally could not occur inside the test file that was supposed to cover this function. This is a green-suite, untested-boundary problem, not a missing-test problem in the naive sense — the module had tests for the function, just not for the input space that breaks it. The Phase 3 reviewer proved it with a failing test before reporting it, per the TDD review discipline, which is the mechanism that caught what the test suite's existence alone did not.

The same review also caught a pure CSS cascade defect (F2: the opener was tappable but inert at <=480px because two later rules at equal specificity silently won) and noted that acceptance item 5 ("anchors never reach the backend") currently holds by payload shape rather than by the funnel (`historyForBackend`) that was built to guarantee it — a tested no-op guarding nothing, until something someday routes real history through the client.

### 4. process friction

- Subagent report-file writes were blocked by a harness policy on every single dispatch this round, not intermittently. All three wave reports and the review had to come back as reply text and get hand-transcribed into the ledger by the controller. This is a 100% failure rate on a documented part of the process (brief step: "write your full report to report-N.md"), not a one-off glitch.
- `agent-browser`'s screenshot command writes to the process cwd on a bare filename, and dropped a stray PNG into the repo root twice during the round.
- The owner revised D1 mid-round, from "sidebar always" to "sidebar >=1200px, under-board below," after the controller actually measured the board's CSS sizing formula and found its own earlier claim ("it will be cramped at 1512") was wrong — the board there is height-bound, not width-bound. This wasn't a process failure so much as evidence that a controller's unverified spatial claim shouldn't be treated as fact when it's cheap to check the actual CSS.

## Learnings

Each rule below is tied to the incident that earned it, and tagged for where it should live.

1. **When a brief says "kill only what you started," name the exact mechanism (PID from a captured launch, not a process-name pattern) — never leave "how" to be inferred from "what."** Earned by: the stack kill. `pkill`/`killall` on a process name is unsafe by construction whenever two same-binary stacks can coexist, which is always true in this repo's alt-port worktree pattern. → **standing rule, both `CLAUDE.md` (hard-rules section) and the `build-round` skill's Phase 4 template**: every brief that spins up a server must capture and record the PID(s) at launch and kill only those PIDs; `pkill node`, `pkill vite`, or any name-based kill is disallowed language in a brief, full stop.

2. **Icon/asset work needs a geometry brief and a falsification test on iteration one, not after eight failed attempts.** Earned by: the icon. Adjective briefs ("make it look like X") under-specify shape for a model that has no persistent visual memory of "X" across renders. A geometry brief (named parts, proportions, what's NOT there) plus a hard self-test ("if it can be read as Y, Z, or W, it failed") is what actually worked, and it was available from the start. → **standing rule for future visual/icon work in the `build-round` skill's visual-wave guidance**: any brief asking for an original glyph/icon must specify geometry (named parts + what must NOT appear) and a falsification test, not adjectives, before the first render.

3. **The controller must render and judge visual output itself, not relay the building subagent's self-assessment.** Earned by: the icon (two declared-done failures caught only once the controller started judging renders in its own context) — and also by wave 3's report, where the controller independently re-ran gates rather than trusting the reported counts. → **standing rule, already partially covered by "verify every subagent claim independently" but worth stating explicitly for visual work specifically**: a subagent's "this looks right" is a claim, not a verification: render it and look, every time, for visual deliverables.

4. **A green suite is not evidence against a bug in the specific input space the suite didn't cover — before accepting a pure-logic module as done, ask what input collisions the fixtures conveniently avoid.** Earned by: F1 (`focusKey` collision, hidden because every test used distinct fixture text). → **standing rule for the `build-round` skill's TDD phases (both wave-1-style logic implementation and Phase 3 review)**: when a function's identity/dedup logic is keyed off content (not off a guaranteed-unique id), the test plan must include a same-content, different-position case, not just distinct-content cases. This is a repo-specific enough pattern (focus keys, dedup keys, anchor keys) that it's worth naming explicitly rather than trusting "write good tests" to reach it.

5. **A tested guard with no live caller is a false sense of safety — flag it as a note, don't let it read as done.** Earned by: F3 (`historyForBackend` fully tested, zero callers, the real protection is payload shape). This one is lower stakes and already correctly triaged as a note-not-blocker by the reviewer — it doesn't need a new rule, just continued vigilance that "the function is tested" and "the invariant is enforced" are different claims. → **one-off**, already handled correctly by the review's own severity call; worth keeping in mind, not worth codifying.

6. **Subagent full-report writes are currently blocked by harness policy at a 100% rate this round — stop treating that as an exception path.** Earned by: every wave and the review all being transcribed by hand. The `build-round` skill's return-format instruction ("max 10 lines in reply, full report to report-N.md") assumes the file write succeeds; it did not, once, this round. → **standing rule, skill-level**: either the skill should stop asking subagents to write report files and instead ask for a slightly longer structured reply (accepting the context cost), or the controller should budget for hand-transcription as the default outcome rather than a fallback. Right now the skill's own documented mechanism doesn't work and every round is silently working around it. This is worth fixing once rather than re-discovering every round.

7. **`agent-browser` screenshot commands need an explicit output path, not a bare filename, in every brief that uses them.** Earned by: two stray PNGs landing in the repo root this round. → **standing rule, small**: add "always pass an absolute path to agent-browser screenshot commands, never a bare filename" to the `build-round` skill's Phase 4 template and to CLAUDE.md's context-economy list.

8. **Unverified spatial/sizing claims from the controller should be checked against the actual CSS before they drive a decision, not asserted from memory.** Earned by: the D1 revision, where the controller's own earlier "cramped at 1512" claim was wrong until someone measured `min(76vmin, calc(100vh - 426px), 1100px)`. This one is closer to good practice than a rule violation — the correction happened within the same round, cheaply, and got recorded with its reasoning. → **one-off / good practice reminder**, not worth a standing rule beyond what "verify claims before committing to a decision" already covers.

## Bottom line

The process changes made after the 2026-07-17 round (plan/execute split, ledger-carried state, independent verification, owner-approval pauses before `src/`) all held under a live test this round, including surviving a hard main rebase and a cross-workstream `GamePage.tsx` merge. The failures were not in that scaffolding — they were a brief that specified an outcome without a mechanism (the stack kill), a review-catchable gap in test-input coverage that the review did in fact catch (F1), and a controller judgment lag on visual work that took nine iterations too long to correct (the icon). The two structural fixes worth carrying forward as standing rules are the PID-only kill instruction and the geometry-not-adjectives brief pattern for icon work; the report-file-write failure is worth fixing at the skill level since it is now a 100%-reproducing gap, not an edge case.
